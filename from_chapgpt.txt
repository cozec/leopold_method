Leopold Aschenbrenner’s stock-picking approach behind Situational Awareness, the core idea is quite different from conventional value investing.

His basic framework
Start with a big technological thesis
His central thesis is that AI capability will continue scaling rapidly.
Therefore, the question becomes: What physical infrastructure is required to make that happen?
This leads him toward semiconductors, memory, power, data centers, networking, and AI compute.

Find the bottlenecks

Rather than simply buying the most famous AI companies, he looks for areas where AI growth could run into scarcity.

A simplified chain is:

AI models → GPUs → HBM/memory → networking → data centers → electricity → cooling/power infrastructure

The companies sitting at a critical bottleneck can potentially capture disproportionate economics.

Look for companies where the market underestimates the bottleneck

This is probably the most interesting part of his strategy.

For example, his fund's positions have included:

MU — Micron
SNDK — SanDisk
TSM — TSMC
BE — Bloom Energy
NBIS — Nebius
IREN — IREN
semiconductor/infrastructure companies

His portfolio became extremely concentrated in memory and AI infrastructure. As of June 30, 2026, reported 13F data showed approximately 28% in SNDK and 28% in MU, with the top five positions accounting for about 78% of reported long equity holdings.

Think in terms of physical constraints

This is where his strategy differs from simply saying "AI is going to grow."

For example:

AI growth → enormous GPU deployment → enormous HBM demand → limited HBM supply → pricing power → potentially extraordinary semiconductor profits.

Similarly:

AI data centers → enormous electricity requirements → grid/power bottleneck → companies providing power infrastructure may benefit.

Bloom Energy was an example of his power-infrastructure thesis.

Concentrate when conviction is high

This is perhaps the most controversial aspect.

He wasn't running a traditional diversified ETF portfolio. His fund made very concentrated bets, sometimes combined with leverage and options. That dramatically increases both upside and downside.

The July 2026 AI selloff demonstrated the risk: reports indicate that the fund experienced a major drawdown after large positions in semiconductor stocks fell sharply.

A useful way to reproduce the strategy

For your own ETF/stock research, I would translate Aschenbrenner's approach into this 5-step screen:

Step	Question
1. Mega-trend	What technology could grow 5–10×?
2. Bottleneck	What scarce resource limits that growth?
3. Supplier	Which companies control that bottleneck?
4. Economics	Can scarcity create pricing power/margin expansion?
5. Valuation	Has the market already priced in the growth?

For example, AI memory:

AI scaling
↓
More GPUs / accelerators
↓
Much more HBM + DRAM
↓
Memory supply constrained
↓
MU / SNDK / SK Hynix benefit
↓
Potential earnings acceleration
↓
Then ask: How much of that future growth is already reflected in the stock price?

That's much closer to an Aschenbrenner-style investment process than simply buying NVDA because AI is growing.
